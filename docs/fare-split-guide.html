Plain-language operations guide

Where does the fare go?

FlexiGo can run the same customer trip in two different ways. The important difference is who provides the car and who pays its operating costs.

Start here

One customer price. Two fulfillment models.

The customer sees one total. Behind that total, FlexiGo uses either a driver-owned car split or a FlexiGo fleet operating calculation. The correct model is selected by an admin before the ride is finalized.

Important: a collected tax is not FlexiGo income, and an airport fee is not automatically FlexiGo income. Their treatment depends on the configured rule and the real party that owes the charge.
Model A

Driver-owned car

The driver brings and operates their own eligible vehicle. The driver normally bears vehicle ownership costs such as fuel, maintenance, insurance, and depreciation.

  • FlexiGo calculates the customer fare.
  • FlexiGo keeps a configured percentage of the trip fare, plus the booking fee.
  • The driver receives the rest of the trip fare.
  • Airport reimbursement is added to the driver when configured that way.
  • Tax is collected for remittance, not treated as driver or FlexiGo earnings.
Driver payout = trip fare − FlexiGo commission + airport reimbursement
Model B

FlexiGo fleet car

FlexiGo supplies the car and pays for operating it. The driver is paid compensation for completing the trip instead of receiving a percentage of the fare.

  • FlexiGo pays the driver a configured trip amount plus per-mile pay.
  • FlexiGo pays fuel, maintenance/depreciation reserve, and payment processing.
  • The key business measure is estimated contribution, not commission.
  • The ride is healthy only when contribution remains positive after real operating costs.
Estimated contribution = customer total − tax − driver compensation − fuel − vehicle reserve − processing − airport cost

Industry context

What other mobility businesses show

Large ride-hail platforms

Companies such as Uber and Lyft generally calculate rider prices and driver earnings separately. Their effective platform take can vary by market, incentives, insurance, fees, and ride conditions. A single published commission percentage is usually not the whole story.

Commission marketplaces

Many aggregator models retain a platform fee or commission while independent drivers provide the car and absorb vehicle costs. FlexiGo’s driver-owned model follows this family, but keeps the split explicit in the admin portal.

Direct-to-driver model

Namma Yatri publicly describes a zero-commission, direct-payment model. It is a useful transparency benchmark, but it requires another way to fund technology, support, compliance, and operations.

Reference points: Namma Yatri describes direct payment and zero commission. For FlexiGo, company-fleet costs must still be covered from each fleet ride’s contribution margin.
